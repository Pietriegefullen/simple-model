\subsection{Pathways}

The model takes into account $N_P$ pathways, each characterized by a reaction equation and associated with a group of microbes.
Each pathway is characterized by a reaction equation.

\subsection{System}

The system is composed of several substances and microbe populations, both changing over time.
The amount of the $i$-th substance (chemical or microbial species) that is present in the system at time $t$ is 
\begin{equation}
S_i = S_i(t).
\end{equation}
The amount of biomass for the microbial pools are given by 
\begin{equation}
M_j = M_j(t).
\end{equation}
The entire system state is described by the array
\begin{equation}
\bm{S} = \begin{bmatrix}
        S_0\\
        S_1\\
        \ldots
        S_{N_S}\\
        M_0\\
        M_1\\
        \ldots\\
        M_{N_P}
        \end{bmatrix}
\end{equation}
\\

The microbes' metabolism is composed of an anabolistic and a catabolistic fraction.
The reaction equation describes the catabolism. 
In addition, the microbes use one substance as carbon source for the anabolism.
The carbon-use-efficiency $\cuej$ indicates the fraction of carbon turnover which goes into growth.
Hence, the carbon turnover of the total metabolism is composed of the stoichiometric coefficient for the respective carbon source and additional terms describing the anabolism, i.e.
\begin{equation}
\cuej = \frac{A_j}{A_j + |\Gamma_j|}, 
\end{equation}
where $|\Gamma_j|$ is the stoichiometric coefficient of the substance used as carbon source by the microbes of the $j$-th pathway.
Rearranging yields the effective stoichiometric contribution of the anabolism for the $j$-th pathway
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{j} = |\Gamma_j| \frac{\cuej}{1 - \cuej}.
\end{equation}
This term describes the reduction of substance used as carbon source for the anabolism by the $j$-th pathway's microbes.
\\

The evolution of the system is described via the rates of change
\begin{align}
\frac{\mathrm{d}S_i}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \sum_{j=1}^{N_P} M_j v_j  \left(\gamma_{ij} - \alpha_{ij}\right),\\
\frac{\mathrm{d}M_j}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \sum_{j=1}^{N_P} M_j v_j \left(\alpha_{ij} - \delta \right)
\end{align}
for the substances and microbes, respectively.
The stoichiometric coefficients for the $i$-th substance, $\gamma_{ij}$, are negative for reactants and positive for products.
Furthermore,
\begin{equation}
\alpha_{ij} = \begin{cases} A_j & \mathrm{if}\ i = \mathrm{carbon\ source\ index}\\
                        0 & \mathrm{otherwise},
            \end{cases}
\end{equation}
describes the anabolism, transferring carbon from the source to the driving microbe group of the $j$-th pathway.
The diminishing rate $\delta$ is constant for all microbe groups.
\\


The $j$-th pathway's turnover rate is governed by the velocity
\begin{equation}
v_j = v_{\mathrm{max},j} \cdot f_{\mathrm{MM},j} \cdot f_{\mathrm{thermo},j}\cdot f_{\mathrm{inhib},j},
\end{equation}
with the constant maximum turnover rate $v_{\mathrm{max}}$ and the time-dependent dimensionless factors $f_{\cdot,j}$ with values between $0$ and $1$, summarizing the effects of kinetics (Michaelis-Menten and inhibition) and thermodynamics at time $t$.

The forward and inverse kinetics are modelled by the factors
\begin{equation}
f_{\mathrm{MM},j} = \prod_{i = 1}^{N_{\mathrm{S}}} \frac{S_i}{S_i + K_{m,ij}}
\end{equation}
and 
\begin{equation}
f_{\mathrm{inhib},j} = \prod_{i = 1}^{N_{\mathrm{S}}} \frac{S_i}{S_i + K_{\mathrm{inhib},ij}},
\end{equation}
respectively, where $K_{m,ij}$ and $K_{\mathrm{inhib}, ij}$ are the half-saturation coefficients with respect to the $i$-th substance in the $j$-th pathway.
If the $i$-th pool is irrelevant for the $j$-th pathway, $K_{m,ij} = 0$.
Numerics: Add $\varepsilon = 10^{-12}$ in the denominator, to avoid division by zero.

The thermodynamics is taken into account by the factor
\begin{equation}
f_{\mathrm{thermo},j} = 1 - e^{\frac{\min(0, \Delta G_{r,j} - \Delta G_{r,\min})}{RT}},
\end{equation}
with the energy
\begin{equation}
\Delta G_{r,j} =  R T \log Q_j + \sum_{i = 1}^{N_P} \gamma_{ij}\Delta G_{f,i}
\end{equation}
defined via the reaction quotient of the species' activities $a_i$ for each pathway,
\begin{equation}
Q_j = \prod_{i =1}^{N_P} a_{i}^{\gamma_{ij}},
\end{equation}
and the constant formation energies $\Delta G_{f,i}$.
The Gibbs minimum $\Delta G_{r,\min} = -26 \cdot 10^3 \_ \frac{\mathrm{kJ}}{\mathrm{mol}}$ defines the boundary of thermodynamic viability.
\\

The system behaviour is parametrised by a set of model parameters $\bm{\theta}$
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{d} \bm{S}}{\mathrm{d}t} = f(t, \bm{S}; \bm{\theta})
\end{equation}
and can be integrated numerically using an initial system state $\bm{S}_0$ to yield $\bm{S}(t)$.
